# Narrative and animation review

Use the author's research question as the reference point. The author may prefer a different structure; explain tradeoffs and preserve explicit decisions.

## Questions that improve a draft

- What new understanding or ability should the audience retain?
- What does this page contribute to that question: motivation, necessary background, mechanism, evidence, interpretation, or an implication?
- Is the notation introduced at the moment an audience can see its referent?
- Are several diagrams views of one system? Show the relation before displaying separate views.
- Is this complexity needed for a new insight? What is the smallest case that demonstrates the principle?
- Does a transition preserve a recognizable object, or require the audience to reconstruct the connection?
- Which claims are established in the cited source, which are calculations for this talk, and which have not been cleared for disclosure?

## Concrete revisions

**Notation appears before a reason to use it.** Start with a physical question and a visible effect. Introduce the representation when a calculation or construction needs it. A technical tutorial can move to backup material.

**A symbol appears detached from the figure.** Highlight the corresponding row, site, region, or operation; generate its label from that object. When assembling a formula, move copies of labels into its terms and retain consistent colors.

**Two panels look like two different models.** Begin with one physical unit cell and its degrees of freedom. Separate them into views through an animation or an explicit common-cell outline, then assemble the interactions.

**Repeated complex examples consume the story.** Demonstrate the simplest one fully. For subsequent examples, state what changes, show a compact transition, and devote detailed animation to the new phenomenon.

**A connection vanishes between sections.** Retain the earlier object and transform its role. For example, a generated geometric support can persist while a conserved quantity is placed on it. The scientific connection must first be verified.

**A useful frame appears and immediately disappears.** Hold it at a slide pause, then explain the next operation. A manually gated presentation allows talking time without slow animations.

**Two observables share a mechanism.** Describe the shared construction and each observable's distinct interpretation. Only show an inclusion/equivalence statement when its precise mathematical objects and assumptions support it.

## Independent reviewer brief

Provide the author-approved seed, audience, decisions and disclosure boundaries; the actual scene list; primary-source pointers; and key-frame sheets plus relevant videos. Review what rendered. Return severity, exact scene/beat, observable issue, and a minimal remedy. Distinguish a scientific error from a narrative choice or a visual preference. Do not expose private materials in the report.

Automatic media and geometry checks support this review. They do not test scientific validity, semantic continuity, cognitive load, intentional overlaps, or playback on the presentation machine.
